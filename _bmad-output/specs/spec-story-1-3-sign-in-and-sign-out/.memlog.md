- (question) OQ-1 which installation identifier names the cookie? (a) a random identifier the installation object creates once and keeps; (b) derived from the recorded public address; (c) a deploy-time configuration value. Recommend (a): unique for two installations of one build on one host with different ports, unchanged when the public address changes (story 1.15), no deploy step, same on both hosts
- (question) OQ-2 counting rules the story leaves open: (i) does a per-email count expire without a success? (ii) how does the per-address wait grow once 20 failures fall within 15 minutes? (iii) are attempts during a wait counted? (iv) does a success lower the address count? (v) how is an attempt carrying a valid marker counted? Recommend: (i) clears 15 min after its wait ended, as D-3; (ii) failures counted in a rolling 15 min, wait = 1 min x 2^(n-20) capped at 15 min after the last failure, (n-5) for email; (iii) refused and not counted, as D-3; (iv) the successful attempt is removed from the address count, the rest stays; (v) counted per address only
- (question) OQ-3 what is the signed marker and how long does it last? (a) an HttpOnly Secure __Host- cookie holding the account identifier and an expiry, signed with HMAC-SHA-256 under a random key kept in the installation object; (b) a random token whose hash is stored per account. Lifetime and scope options: 30 days, 1 year, no end; one marker per browser or one per account per browser. Recommend (a), 1 year renewed at each sign-in, kept on sign-out, one marker per browser for the account last signed in: 'signed' in CA-44 points to (a), and it needs no storage per browser
- (question) OQ-4 session lifetime before story 1.6: (a) a browser-session cookie and no server expiry; (b) cookie Max-Age 30 days, the server records sign-in and last-use times and refuses a session 30 days after sign-in (CA-47), the idle limit (CA-18) waits for story 1.6; (c) Max-Age 30 days, nothing enforced. - (question) OQ-5 concurrent scrypt hashing against the 128 MB Worker limit: (a) one hash at a time per Worker isolate (a queue), same for real and stand-in hashes; (b) no limit, relying on Cloudflare spreading requests; (c) a cap with extra attempts refused as busy. Recommend (a): no memory failure, timing parity kept because both cases queue alike, and 5 queued hashes still finish in about 2 s
