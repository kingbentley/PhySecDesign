## Survey records and photos

- A survey note belongs to one floor and has a title, installation notes, labeled measurements, photos, and either a same-floor device anchor or a geographic point. Invalid or foreign device anchors are rejected. Stored titles are trimmed/truncated to 100 characters, note text preserves newlines up to 5,000 characters, measurement labels allow 80 characters, and measurement values are numeric 0–1,000,000 in m/cm/mm/ft/in. Evidence: site-studio/src/projectModel.js:267, site-studio/src/projectModel.js:278, site-studio/src/projectModel.js:283; site-studio/src/survey.js:8.
- The UI supports creating a map pin or device note, editing and saving a note, adding/removing measurements, adding multiple photos, previewing/removing individual photos, locating a saved note, and confirming note deletion. The note editor has an explicit Save/Cancel flow. Evidence: site-studio/src/App.jsx:373, site-studio/src/App.jsx:384, site-studio/src/App.jsx:405; site-studio/src/SurveyInspector.jsx:46, site-studio/src/SurveyInspector.jsx:73, site-studio/src/SurveyInspector.jsx:101, site-studio/src/SurveyInspector.jsx:133, site-studio/src/SurveyInspector.jsx:160.
- Device-anchored notes follow device coordinates. Removing a device converts its notes to point anchors at that device's last position and retains their content; connected cable routes are removed. Evidence: site-studio/src/survey.js:15, site-studio/src/survey.js:20; site-studio/src/App.jsx:213.
- New image attachments are processed in the browser: JPEG/PNG/WebP signatures are checked, an image is decoded, and a canvas creates a metadata-free JPEG preview at quality 0.82. Candidate maximum edges are 1,600, 1,280, 960, and 640 pixels until the encoded size fits. These are bounded previews, not archival originals. Evidence: site-studio/src/survey.js:27, site-studio/src/survey.js:34, site-studio/src/survey.js:55.

## Printable PDF delivery

- Export offers current floor or all floors; US Letter or A4 landscape; combined visibility, combined DORI, or no coverage; and optional survey photos. Preparation shows progress, supports cancellation, and provides a page selector and previous/next preview before PDF download. Editable JSON backup is a separate action. Evidence: site-studio/src/ReportExportPanel.jsx:36, site-studio/src/ReportExportPanel.jsx:62, site-studio/src/ReportExportPanel.jsx:74, site-studio/src/ReportExportPanel.jsx:109.
- For each selected floor the packet builds a labeled plan, equipment/cable schedules, optional security-zone requirements, and survey notes with measurements/photos. Equipment includes ID, type, name/model, mounting/installation notes, height where applicable, and proposed/existing status. Routes include endpoints, cable category, horizontal drawn distance, entered slack, and planned distance. Long schedules and notes flow to additional pages. Evidence: site-studio/src/designReport.js:78, site-studio/src/designReport.js:140, site-studio/src/designReport.js:169, site-studio/src/designReport.js:177, site-studio/src/designReport.js:255.
- Plan sheets draw the imported image, devices, routes, zones, walls/obstructions, and survey references; add a metric scale bar, north arrow, legend, and filename/PDF sheet identity. Floors without a supplied drawing receive a schematic, and image failures produce warnings/marked fallback content. The renderer uses drawing and geometry, not live basemap tiles. Evidence: site-studio/src/reportPlan.js:141, site-studio/src/reportPlan.js:169, site-studio/src/reportPlan.js:222, site-studio/src/reportPlan.js:275; site-studio/src/designReport.js:44, site-studio/src/designReport.js:234; site-studio/README.md:54.

This is planning coverage, not an engineering guarantee. Evidence: site-studio/src/reportPlan.js:18, site-studio/src/reportPlan.js:201, site-studio/src/reportPlan.js:219.

Each page becomes an RGB JPEG at quality 0.94, serialized into PDF 1.4. The output is a static raster-page document; selectable/searchable text or editable vector drawing layers are not produced by this writer. ## Capacity planning

- Capacity aggregates the whole property's cameras, switches, and network endpoints, retaining building/floor labels for rows and Locate actions. Camera inputs are average bitrate, recorded-time percentage, and switch-side PoE allocation; project input is retention days. Evidence: site-studio/src/capacity.js:60, site-studio/src/capacity.js:67; site-studio/src/CapacityPanel.jsx:102, site-studio/src/CapacityPanel.jsx:108, site-studio/src/CapacityPanel.jsx:177.
- Storage uses decimal TB: bitrate Mbps × (recorded percentage / 100) × retention days × 86,400 / 8,000,000. Aggregate bandwidth sums each camera's full configured stream bitrate regardless of its recording percentage. Evidence: site-studio/src/capacity.js:54, site-studio/src/capacity.js:134; site-studio/src/CapacityPanel.jsx:110.
- Every network endpoint consumes one copper access port, including locally powered endpoints whose PoE allocation is zero. A direct same-floor Cat6 route assigns an endpoint only when exactly one distinct switch is linked. Multiple switch candidates and absence of a direct Cat6 route are explicit unassigned reasons. Fiber/security routes do not assign copper access demand. Each drawn switch-to-switch Cat6 route consumes one port at each end. Evidence: site-studio/src/capacity.js:79, site-studio/src/capacity.js:97, site-studio/src/CapacityPanel.jsx:134.
- Per-switch checks compare assigned endpoint count plus drawn copper uplinks with copper ports, powered endpoint count with PoE ports, allocated watts with total PoE budget, and each endpoint's watts with the per-port ceiling. Unassigned demand remains in project totals and is shown separately; a within-limits switch label refers only to its assigned load. Evidence: site-studio/src/capacity.js:116, site-studio/src/capacity.js:123, site-studio/src/capacity.js:134; site-studio/src/CapacityPanel.jsx:141, site-studio/src/CapacityPanel.jsx:160.
- Defaults: 30 retention days; switches 8 copper ports, 8 PoE ports, 60 W total and 30 W per port; generic cameras 4 Mbps, 100% recording, 15.4 W; the 8 MP bullet catalog variant 8 Mbps and 30 W; IP intercoms default to networked and 15.4 W. Cameras always normalize as networked. Other types can be enabled as networked. Evidence: site-studio/src/capacity.js:21, site-studio/src/capacity.js:25; site-studio/src/CapacityPanel.jsx:59.

Existing devices get a separate count and zero purchased quantity; route quantities include saved slack. Equipment and cable unit costs accept project-specific USD overrides. ## Local persistence and portable projects

- The application stores one current draft in IndexedDB, database physec-site-studio, schema version 1, drafts object store, current key. Autosave waits 350 ms after edits; it saves project and named revisions, not undo/redo stacks. UI exposes loading/saving/saved/error states. Evidence: site-studio/src/projectStorage.js:5, site-studio/src/projectStorage.js:39, site-studio/src/projectStorage.js:75, site-studio/src/projectStorage.js:114, site-studio/src/projectStorage.js:128.
- Writes are queued and normalized snapshots are captured before writing. Initial restore completes before a write; a failed initial read prevents overwriting the unread draft. A failed write does not permanently poison later queued saves. The UI instructs exporting after save failure. Evidence: site-studio/src/projectStorage.js:45, site-studio/src/projectStorage.js:104, site-studio/src/projectStorage.js:132; site-studio/src/App.jsx:700.
- Portable format is physec-studio version 2. Supported v1 projects migrate into one building/floor, preserving plan/devices while adopting later defaults. Normalization rebuilds known fields rather than copying arbitrary input. It retains buildings/floors, plan image and PDF page identity, devices, routes, barriers, zones, survey notes/photos, coverage area and common target height, costs, capacity, estimates, and named revisions. Evidence: site-studio/src/projectModel.js:341, site-studio/src/projectModel.js:351, site-studio/src/projectModel.js:383, site-studio/src/projectModel.js:402.
- Local assets are accepted only through an explicit local-assets option; portable plan images must be embedded PNG/JPEG/WebP. Export fetches each distinct local /assets/ URL once and embeds it into every relevant current/revision floor. Named revisions contain full design snapshots, so repeated image/photo strings can contribute repeatedly to serialized backup size. Evidence: site-studio/src/projectModel.js:55, site-studio/src/projectModel.js:454. Repeated-serialization consequence is an inference from the nested snapshot shape.
- Import and export both enforce 100 × 1024 × 1024 byte project-file limits. Export includes revisions; import parses and normalizes before replacing the working draft and clears session undo/redo through replacement history. This is replacement, not a project merge. Evidence: site-studio/src/projectModel.js:10; site-studio/src/App.jsx:500, site-studio/src/App.jsx:508; site-studio/src/projectStorage.js:79; site-studio/src/projectHistory.js:11.

These are validation limits, not tested production performance guarantees. Evidence: site-studio/src/projectModel.js:57, site-studio/src/projectModel.js:154, site-studio/src/projectModel.js:357, site-studio/src/projectModel.js:362, site-studio/src/projectModel.js:366, site-studio/src/projectModel.js:386.

## Shared review and approvals

- Share & review starts from a named revision and publishes a fixed review snapshot. An owner reopening the same review cannot overwrite its design; a new revision needs a separate review. Reviewers can choose a floor, place numbered floor-pin comments, and choose Approved or Changes requested. Decisions are timestamped and repeated identical decisions are deduplicated. Evidence: site-studio/src/RevisionPanel.jsx:128; site-studio/src/RevisionReview.jsx:251, site-studio/src/RevisionReview.jsx:293; site-studio/server/reviewApi.mjs:119, site-studio/server/reviewApi.mjs:178.
- Shared content is narrower than the full named revision. It retains project/building/floor identity, plans, routes, barriers, zones, and device id/type/name/location/status/bearing. It excludes survey notes/photos, equipment installation notes/model/requirements, detailed camera optics and stream settings, coverage boundary/target settings, capacity settings, costs, estimate lines, and revision history. The review renderer shows floor layout rather than analysis/estimate results. Evidence: site-studio/src/reviewModel.js:11, site-studio/src/reviewModel.js:30; site-studio/src/RevisionLayout.jsx:18; site-studio/tests/review-api.test.mjs:24.
- Owner flow collects name/email and creates a separate bearer link for each reviewer. UI offers copying the link and explicitly asks the designer to send it. There is no email delivery or verified recipient login in this flow; possession of the link permits responding as that named reviewer. Another device must be able to reach the server address used to create the link. Evidence: site-studio/src/RevisionReview.jsx:157, site-studio/src/RevisionReview.jsx:188, site-studio/src/RevisionReview.jsx:197; site-studio/src/reviewClient.js:31; site-studio/README.md:75, site-studio/README.md:79.

Reviewer links carry access in the URL fragment; requests send Authorization headers. Server storage hashes owner/reviewer bearer values rather than persisting the raw values. No actual credentials or live storage were read during this extraction.
