# Closet and port planning reference

Research date: 2026-11-17 (America/Chicago).

Purpose: narrow primary-source grounding for a POC closet and manual port-schedule recommendation. Product documentation illustrates useful distinctions; it does not establish universal hardware limits or accepted Site Studio scope. Recommendations below remain subject to the user's PRD decision.

## Evidence

- **Group equipment without losing its identity.** CCTV Design Tool's 30 April 2026 release describes device assemblies that present several devices together while retaining individual components in the BOM and network diagram. This supports compact grouping, but does not itself specify a closet hierarchy. [Official changelog, Device Assembly](https://www.cctvdesigntool.com/changelog)
- **Port types differ within and between switches.** Axis documents PoE-capable RJ45 ports, non-PoE/uplink RJ45 ports and SFP slots across its switch models. Port capability and capacity therefore need a model-specific or explicitly entered basis. [Axis switch documentation, Product overview](https://help.axis.com/en-us/axis-t8524-poe%2B-network-switch)
- **Power values have different meanings.** Axis distinguishes per-port power from the total switch PoE budget. Its current appliance manual separately exposes requested, allocated and consumed power, with several allocation methods. These are not interchangeable planning inputs. [Axis powering calculation guidance, page 8](https://www.axis.com/files/acad/ar_top_10_installation_challenges_49690_en_1211_lo.pdf), [S2216 Mk II manual, About the built-in switch and Power management](https://help.axis.com/en-us/axis-s2216-mk-ii)
- **Controller terminals are not Ethernet ports.** The A1601 documentation distinguishes its RJ45 network connector from reader, door and relay terminal blocks. Secure Entry also distinguishes wired reader/REX connections from IP readers, so device category alone cannot determine the network connection. [A1601, Connectors](https://help.axis.com/en-us/axis-a1601), [Secure Entry, IP reader and Add a REX device](https://help.axis.com/en-us/axis-camera-station-secure-entry)
- **Patching is part of the cabling path.** Fluke Networks describes a channel between active devices that includes patch cords, patch panels, outlets and equipment cords as applicable. This supports recording passive termination references separately from the active switch endpoint. This note does not adopt the article's testing standards or distance limits. [Channel, Permanent Link, Patch Cords, MPTL, E2E](https://www.flukenetworks.com/blog/cabling-chronicles/channel-permanent-link-patch-cords-mptl-e2e-oh-my)

## Bounded POC recommendation

1. **Named closet or equipment location:** stable ID, name, floor/location and notes, with an optional cabinet asset. Group existing switches, controllers, recorders, power supplies and passive equipment by reference; retain their separate IDs, models and quantities. A location is not automatically another billable device. Keep physical containment distinct from the closet that serves a remote endpoint.
2. **Manual switch port table:** explicit port label, interface type, known PoE capability, and allocation such as endpoint, uplink, spare, reserved, unavailable or unknown. Assigned rows reference the connected device/interface and, where present, its cable record. Include notes and the source of entered capability data. Do not assume every connector is an independent usable port or that all ports support PoE.
3. **Useful checks:** flag conflicting assignments, missing references and incompatible known port types. Show assigned, reserved, unavailable and remaining known ports separately. Unspecified capacity cannot produce an exact spare count; an unassigned or unverified connection remains visibly unresolved. Uplinks consume their actual assigned ports, rather than an invented allowance.
4. **Modest PoE planning:** record known total switch budget, known per-port limits and each planned load/allocation with its basis and source. Compare compatible values only. Show known subtotal and unknown-load count; incomplete inputs produce an incomplete assessment, not a pass. Typical consumption is not a guaranteed maximum. Do not silently convert device-side watts into switch-side allocation or infer a budget from port count.
5. **Separate terminal and passive references:** reader/contact/lock/REX links can name the controller and terminal/channel without consuming an Ethernet port unless that component is explicitly an IP endpoint. Permit optional patch-panel, outlet and termination labels on a cable/assignment; passive patching is not an extra active network endpoint. Preserve cable route length and explicit allowances without deriving them from closet membership.

The smallest useful deliverable is a closet equipment list plus a manual switch port schedule in the editable project and relevant design/closeout exports. No live discovery, switch configuration, automatic port assignment, full rack elevation, patch-panel connectivity graph, universal channel limits, electrical certification or standards obligation is proposed. Planned assignments and recorded as-built evidence must remain distinguishable.
